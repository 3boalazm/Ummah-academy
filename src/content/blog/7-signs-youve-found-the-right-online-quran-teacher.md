A good Quran teacher shapes how quickly you improve, whether you actually enjoy the process, and whether you stick with it past the first month. Here are 7 concrete signs — not vague “good vibes” — that tell you a teacher is genuinely worth committing to.

## 1. They Correct You Immediately, Not at the End

A strong teacher stops you the moment a Tajweed rule is missed — a shortened Madd, a rushed Ghunnah — rather than letting you finish the page and giving general feedback afterward. Immediate correction is how mistakes don’t get memorized alongside the verse.

## 2. They Explain the “Why,” Not Just the “What”

Anyone can say “say it like this.” A teacher worth keeping explains why a letter is pronounced from a specific point of articulation (Makhraj), or why a particular Noon Sakinah rule applies here and not there — so you start recognizing the pattern yourself instead of only imitating.

## 3. They Have a Real, Verifiable Credential

Ask directly where they studied and what their Ijazah covers. A teacher trained at [Al-Azhar Al-Sharif](/teachers/), with a documented chain of transmission (Sanad), is a fundamentally different level of qualification than someone who simply “grew up reciting.”

## 4. Your Trial Session Feels Like a Real Lesson

A trial session that’s mostly a sales pitch is a red flag. A real trial should function like an actual class — you read, they correct, you leave with at least one specific, actionable note about your recitation.

## 5. They Adjust to How You Learn

Some students need repetition drilled slowly; others pick up rules quickly but need help with consistency. A teacher who uses the same fixed script regardless of who’s in front of them isn’t actually teaching you — they’re reciting a lesson plan at you.

## 6. They Track Your Progress Across Sessions

Real progress requires memory — of what you struggled with last week, what’s improved, and what still needs work. If every session feels like it’s starting from zero, there’s no continuity to your learning.

## 7. They’re Comfortable Being Asked Hard Questions

Whether it’s about a Tajweed rule, an opinion within Fiqh, or how long realistic progress takes, a confident, well-trained teacher welcomes questions rather than deflecting them.
